# Negation overlap: Fiendish Chain versus Breakthrough Skill

Example ID: negation-overlap-001
Concepts: activation legality, existing negation, independent attack restriction, conjunctions.
Intended context: TCG HAT format.
Recorded: 2026-11-07.
Review status: source-backed teaching draft; historical HAT-specific validation remains pending. Do not label this entry as a formally reviewed historical ruling.

## Scenario

The opponent is the turn player and declares an attack with their face-up Stardust Dragon. Skill Drain has already resolved and is applying. The defending player has a legally Set Fiendish Chain. Assume no other effects interfere.

The original spoken scenario called the defending player the turn player. Normalize that inconsistency explicitly: the attacking opponent is the turn player.

## User-facing explanation

You can activate Fiendish Chain targeting Stardust Dragon even though Skill Drain already negates its effects. Fiendish Chain also prevents the targeted monster from attacking. That restriction does not depend on successfully negating the monster's effects again. If Fiendish Chain resolves and remains applicable, Stardust's attack stops.

Compare Breakthrough Skill: you cannot activate it targeting that already-negated Stardust Dragon, because the relevant effect only negates monster effects. It has no independent attack restriction.

Stardust remains an Effect Monster while its effects are negated.

## Teaching requirements

- Address the user's actual confusion: overlapping negation and whether activation is legal.
- Separate activation legality from what happens at resolution.
- Explain the independent attack restriction rather than merely saying Skill Drain does not negate Traps.
- Fiendish Chain uses "also" to connect its negation and attack restriction. The attack restriction is not conditional on fresh negation.
- Do not generalize this to "any effect with two parts can be activated." Check the specific card's activation requirements and rulings. Conjunctions explain resolution dependencies; they are not a universal test for activation legality.
- Breakthrough Skill has a separate Graveyard effect, but that does not add an independent operation to its on-field activation.
- The example assumes Skill Drain is already applying. Do not confuse it with Skill Drain being chained to a legally activated negation effect.
- Do not imply that Fiendish Chain resolves two separate Chain Links or negates an attack by a dedicated attack-negation effect: it makes the monster unable to attack.
